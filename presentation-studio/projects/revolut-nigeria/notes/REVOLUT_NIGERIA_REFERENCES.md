## Academic notes

- **Terminology.** The course lists "Individualism vs. collectivism". Trompenaars and Hampden-Turner's original term is *communitarianism*. The slides use the course term and the notes mention the original. "Objectivity vs. neutrality" is printed as in the course; the course also describes it as Affective vs Neutral (p. 33).
- **No national scores.** No national cultural scores are used for Nigeria. I found no current, verifiable Nigerian Trompenaars scores, and the course frames the model as a profiling lens. Each cultural pattern is stated as a hypothesis to validate locally.
- **Hypothetical case.** Nothing in this presentation describes an actual Revolut product, licence, office, partnership or campaign in Nigeria. All recommendations depend on regulatory approval, local partnerships and local legal assessment.

# Production disclosures

- **AI-generated footage.** The three character shots in the 30-second film (the entrepreneur, the professional and the student) were generated with MiniMax H3 through vidIQ: 768p, 6 s each, upscaled to 1080p. They show fictional people, not real customers or employees.
- **AI voiceover.** vidIQ voiceover, voice "Lily" (British English). No Nigerian-accented voice was available; a Nigerian voice artist would strengthen local relevance in a real campaign.
- **Music and sound effects.** Original, synthesized for this project (numpy). No third-party music.
- **Interface.** The phone interface is a conceptual mock-up with illustrative data. It shows no real Revolut features, and no Revolut logo is used.
- **Labelling.** The film and poster are labelled "Proposed concept — not an official Revolut ad".
