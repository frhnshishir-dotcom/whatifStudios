# Presentation quality standard

Score each criterion from 1 to 5. **Ship at 4+ on every line.** A 5 in visual design does not make up for a 2 in content.

| # | Criterion | 5 means | Automated help |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Visual design | Looks art-directed: one dominant colour, purposeful negative space, a consistent motif, nothing generic | contact sheets |
| 2 | Storytelling | Hook, stakes, three movements, proof, close; one message per slide; titles read as a story on their own | speaker script |
| 3 | Content accuracy | Every number cited and verified; units and years stated; no fabricated quotes | `citation`, `source` checks |
| 4 | Academic relevance | Every rubric criterion and required section is visibly covered; references in the required style | `brief`, `references` checks |
| 5 | Typography | Clear hierarchy (title ≥ 36 pt, body ≥ 14 pt); no widows or broken numbers; consistent title positions | `type-size`, overflow, overlap |
| 6 | Visual assets | Sharp (≥ 110 ppi on slide), licensed and attributed, on-brand; 3D and video earn their place | `image-res`, missing-image |
| 7 | Motion design | 2–3 transition types with clear grammar; builds reveal in reading order; nothing gratuitous | animation walk-through |
| 8 | Consistency | Same grid, chart styling, colours and footers across all slides | geometry checks |
| 9 | Technical execution | QA PASS; the .pptx opens and validates; fonts handled for the presenting PC; PDF matches | `pptx-*`, `html-*` checks |
| 10 | Overall experience | Fits the time slot (±20 %); rehearsable from the notes; the audience leaves with one sentence | `timing` check |
